Multiple Parental Investment and Intergenerational Mobility

Intergenerational human capital, transfers, and bequests

Last updated: 2026-06-30

1 Goal

This project develops and estimates a structural model of intergenerational human capital formation using Swedish data. The model connects pre-birth endowments, parental time investment, financial transfers, bequests, education, and labor-market outcomes in a dynamic framework.

The empirical goal is to decompose how different family resources shape human capital accumulation and later outcomes, then use the estimated model for counterfactual exercises.

2 Current Model

The model below is the current working specification. Several pieces remain provisional, especially the final timing convention, the exact choice set, and the moment vector used in estimation.

2.1 Timing

The provisional timing uses four broad stages.

Period Stage Main object
\(t=1\) Basic education Early human capital and ability measures
\(t=2\) Upper secondary Track, study effort, child work, parental investment
\(t=3\) Young adulthood Tertiary enrollment, work, parental death/bequest risk
\(t=4\) Career Adult human capital and earnings

2.2 State

A compact state vector for child \(i\) in period \(t\) is:

\[ s_{it} = \left( t,\, h_{it},\, e_i^f,\, e_i^m,\, e_{it}^c,\, \eta_i,\, z_{it} \right). \]

Here \(h_{it}\) is human capital, \(e_i^f\) and \(e_i^m\) are parental education, \(e_{it}^c\) is child education or track, \(\eta_i\) is latent ability or endowment, and \(z_{it}\) collects demographics and institutional states.

2.3 Choices

The intended choice vector is broader than the current MATLAB prototype:

\[ d_{it} = \left( \ell_{it}^f,\, \ell_{it}^m,\, x_{it},\, m_{it},\, \tau_{it},\, b_{it} \right). \]

\(\ell_{it}^f\) and \(\ell_{it}^m\) denote parental labor supply, \(x_{it}\) is child study effort, \(m_{it}\) is parental time investment, \(\tau_{it}\) is an inter-vivo transfer, and \(b_{it}\) is a bequest-related resource.

Current MATLAB status: the prototype enumerates 12 terminal choices from father labor supply, mother labor supply, and high/zero transfer. Study time, school track, enrollment, child work, and human-capital transitions are not yet implemented.

2.4 Utility

A general period utility can be written as:

\[ u_t(s_{it},d_{it}) = \omega_p u_p(c_{it}^p,\ell_{it}^f,\ell_{it}^m,m_{it}) + \omega_c u_c(c_{it}^c,x_{it},\ell_{it}^c) + \xi_t(d_{it}). \]

The current terminal-period code uses the placeholder:

\[ u_4(y_p,y_c)=\log(y_p)+\log(y_c), \qquad V_T(y_p,y_c)=\alpha_p \log(y_p)+\alpha_c \log(y_c). \]

2.5 Human Capital Transition

The model needs a period-specific human-capital production function:

\[ h_{i,t+1} = g_t \left( h_{it},\, x_{it},\, m_{it},\, \eta_i,\, e_i^f,\, e_i^m,\, X_{it}; \theta_h \right) + \varepsilon^h_{i,t+1}. \]

A central open decision is whether ability \(\eta_i\) mainly shifts baseline human capital, raises the productivity of investments, or both.

2.6 Dynamic Problem

Given states, choices, transitions, and terminal value, the recursive problem is:

\[ V_t(s_{it}) = \max_{d_{it}\in\mathcal{D}_t(s_{it})} \left\{ u_t(s_{it},d_{it}) + \beta \mathbb{E} \left[ V_{t+1}(s_{i,t+1}) \mid s_{it},d_{it} \right] \right\}. \]

3 Current Estimation Procedure

The current estimation plan is simulated minimum distance / Method of Simulated Moments. For each candidate parameter vector, solve the model, simulate life-cycle histories, construct simulated moments, and compare them to empirical moments.

\[ \widehat{\theta} = \arg\min_{\theta} \left[ m^{data} - m^{sim}(\theta) \right]' W \left[ m^{data} - m^{sim}(\theta) \right]. \]
  1. Choose a candidate parameter vector \(\theta\).
  2. Solve the dynamic model by backward recursion.
  3. Simulate households using the policy rules.
  4. Compute simulated moments using empirical moment definitions.
  5. Evaluate the weighted distance from empirical moments.
  6. Update parameters until the objective and diagnostics are stable.
